## Bond
A bond is a debt instrument issued by governments, corporations, and other organisations to raise capital. When an investor purchases a bond, they pay an initial amount (the bond price) to the issuer in the primary market or to another investor in the secondary market. At maturity, the bondholder receives the bond's face value. Some bonds are "coupon-bearing," entitling the bondholder to periodic interest payments, known as coupons, until the bond matures.

## Carry
The income earned by the bondholder from doing nothing (as time passes). It is equal to the coupon received by the bondholder minus funding costs (if any).

## Duration
A measure of the sensitivity of a bond's price to interest rates. All else equal, longer-maturity bonds have a higher duration than shorter-maturity bonds.

## Face value
When two market participants trade a bond, they agree on the **nominal amount** also called face value to be bought and sold, as well as its price. For most bonds, this nominal amount is the principal that the issuer promises to repay to the holder at maturity, assuming no default.

## Spread
In financial markets, a spread is simply the difference between two prices or two interest rates. In the Eurozone, the two most well-known spreads are the OAT-Bund spread and the BTP-Bund spread.

## Yield curve
In the usual sense, given a universe of bonds (for instance, all bonds issued by the French government, all bonds issued by BNP Paribas), the yield curve represents the plot of each bond's yield to maturity as a function of its maturity.

## Yield-to-maturity (YTM)
The yield to maturity can be a confusing concept. For a zero-coupon bond, the definition is quite intuitive, it is simply the annualised rate of return of the investment if the bondholder keeps the instrument until maturity.

## Zero-coupon bond
A zero-coupon bond (ZC bond) is a debt security that does not pay periodic interest (coupons) to the bondholder. Instead, it is issued at a discount to its face value and pays the full face value at maturity. The bondholder's return is realised entirely from the difference between the purchase price and the amount received at maturity.

For a zero-coupon bond, the YTM is the annualised rate of return if the bondholder keeps the instrument until maturity.
